The game

Five stocks, one armband, ten points a percent

  1. 1

    A position per stock, set by how hard it moves. Keepers are the steadiest names, forwards the ones that swing. You cannot field five forwards even when momentum says you should, and that constraint is the game.

  2. 2

    One pick wears the armband and its stake is doubled. A real position size, not a multiplier bolted onto a scoreboard, so the chain settles exactly what the table shows.

  3. 3

    Ten points a percent. A five percent day is fifty points, the range a real fantasy gameweek lands in. Points are only a presentation of the ratio the contract settles on.

  4. 4

    Every pick is a real swap into a wallet you own, held by a passkey. A whole draft is one batched transaction, so you sign once and never end up holding two picks out of five.

  5. 5

    Twenty-four hours later the contract reads Chainlink, ranks the league, and pays the top three sixty, thirty, ten. Then the next matchday opens.

Straight about what is not here: no round has been played. The wallet holding the float became unreachable before the first one could open, and a round with nothing staked in it cannot settle, by design. So rather than claim a result that did not happen, every step the round depends on runs against live mainnet: a real swap through the real Aerodrome pool via eth_call, and a whole round opened, locked and settled in a multi-block simulation with the clock moved forward onto real feed data. One command, in the repo.
